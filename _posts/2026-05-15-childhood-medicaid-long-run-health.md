From June 2025 to May 2026, I was a research assistant at Loyola University Chicago on a project studying the **long-run effects of childhood Medicaid eligibility on chronic disease biomarkers and mortality after age 50**. The work was presented at **ASHEcon 2026**, the American Society of Health Economists conference.

## The question

When Medicaid eligibility expanded, some children gained access to health coverage that others born a little earlier or in other states did not. Did that early coverage still matter decades later, when those children were in their fifties and sixties?

## What I worked on

- Analyzed biomarker data from venous blood samples in the **Health and Retirement Study** (HRS) to measure chronic-disease risk in adults over 50.
- Implemented a **difference-in-differences** design to estimate whether broader Medicaid eligibility in early childhood reduced chronic-disease risk later in life.
- Analyzed restricted-access HRS mortality data to estimate the long-run effects on mortality.
